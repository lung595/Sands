Option A - Last grains

The hourglass empties onto your screen: a thread of sand falls, heaps up, then slumps into a dune while a few grains roll down.

Why it is not generic: the alert is the end of the hourglass itself, and the falling thread is the thread of the Fil look. No banner, no card.

Replays both views below
MovementThread falls from the top edge (about 1.5 s), a heap grows under it and slumps while the sand spreads from the centre: two fronts run toward the edges with grains rolling ahead of them, and the edges fill last. Ends by itself: hard stop 2.4 s after the first grain lands, about 3 s in all.
Technique for the QML buildOne Canvas in a Loader, created when the alarm rings. The settled dune (grains of 2 to 4 px in 4 tints mixed from primary, on-primary and text, the lit face, the shaded face and foot) is painted once into a still picture. A Timer at 30 Hz, never a looping QML animation, repaints only the outline of the growing sand over that picture and the loose grains; it stops itself at the end.
Drawn once, then kept stillThe whole dune texture. After the last step the Timer is stopped, nothing moves and nothing is redrawn until Stop. On Stop: 150 ms fade, then the Loader is emptied. Nothing exists at rest.
Grains and stepsCounted live under each stage above (grains painted once, most loose grains at one time, steps drawn).
Reduce motionThe settled dune fades in over 150 ms with its full grain texture. No thread, no loose grain, no Timer.
CaptionOn-primary text on the sand. It appears only once sand is behind it. Shades are free everywhere except in the caption band (caption height + 8 px), where they are held to 5:1. The weakest pair is read back from the painted pixels: under each stage, and for the 6 themes in the table at the bottom.
CostNot measured yet. Written estimate: about 90 repaints of one canvas during the movement, more than Today while it moves, the same as Today afterwards (zero). The build story measures it (largest 1 s and core-seconds).
Cost to buildMedium: one pure .js for the surface and the grains (tested), one Canvas component.

Option B - Wind dune

Wind carries the sand in from the left edge: grains hop along the ground, a crest travels across and stops, sand blows off the crest and slides down the steep side.

Why it is not generic: a real dune shape, a long windward slope and a short steep face, built the way wind builds one. The name Rising dune finally says what you see.

Option C - Flat wave

A cartoon dune that lands with weight: it gathers under the edge, springs up well past its height, then thuds down three times, each softer and faster. Every thud throws fine sand off the crest, grains trickle down the slip face, and round grains hop and roll back down the slope.

Why it is not generic: one flat shape drawn like a cartoon cel, alive through squash and stretch, with the weight and the loose grains of sand rather than the swing of water. It is the wave of Today given a body and a pulse, not a sheet sliding up.

Replays both views below
Movement0 to 200 ms, anticipation: a lip of sand shows at the edge, rises to 16 px, dips 6 px and widens by 12 %. 200 to 700 ms, rise: ease-out with one overshoot of about 18 % (back curve, k 2.4), the crest first, the sides 120 ms later, the body leaning toward the crest. 700 to 1680 ms, three beats that speed up, 420, 320 and 240 ms at 20 %, 11 % and 5 %: the height drops in the first 30 % of each beat, like a thud, and builds back over the rest, while the width goes up by the same share, then back; the crest lags 40 ms behind the body and the lean straightens. At the overshoot and at each thud fine sand is thrown off the crest (10, 12, 7 and 4 grains of 2 or 3 px on short arcs), and 7 grains trickle down the slip face between 0.75 and 2.25 s. 2000 ms, settle in 150 ms. Hard stop at 2.4 s. The overshoot and the deepest squash are measured in px under each stage.
Cartoon highlightA rim 3 px thick along the crest, primary mixed toward a Theme role (the lighter of text and on-primary when it can reach 3:1 against primary, the other one otherwise), the share raised until 3:1 is measured; it thickens when the wave squashes. A 1 px base line, primary mixed toward the darker of the two roles, runs under the rim on the flank opposite the crest and gives the cel its volume. 8 round grains of 6 to 8 px, each with its highlight, hop off the crest at the overshoot, twice as high as before; 3 of them fall back and roll down the slope, the others rest on the crest. The shape is a dune ridge: a long slope left of the crest, a slip face half as long on the right.
Sand matterSpecks of 2 or 3 px in two tints (primary mixed toward the rim role at half the rim share, and the base line tint), dense under the crest line and thinning with depth, one for every 5 px of width, 160 at most. They ride the surface and are never drawn in the caption band.

When an app is full screen

By default nothing is drawn over a full-screen app: the sound and the DMS notification tell you, with Stop and +5 min on the card. One setting, off by default, draws the dune over the app anyway. Same rule for the three options.
